# 0016 — The comparison, cut to the part that carries information

## Categorised request

| Kind | Request | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Content** | Remove or rephrase the ten-tool comparison | **cut** — ten rows became four losses |
| **Work** | File the gaps as wayfinder lanes | **done** — map #43, lanes #44–#49 |
| **Lever** | Ponytail, full | applied: rung 1 |

## Ponytail's answer

Rung 1 of the ladder is "does this need to exist at all". The table failed it on
three counts:

1. **It rots.** Every price and product name in it is a fact somebody has to
   re-verify quarterly, and the repo cannot verify any of them.
2. **It argues one way.** Nine of ten rows concluded in this product's favour; a
   comparison where one side always wins is a brochure with a table around it.
3. **The losses are the information.** A reader who needs rule enforcement or git
   history should use the other tool. Four flat sentences say that in a tenth of
   the space and none of the maintenance.

So: the table goes, the four losses stay, the closing paragraph stays.

## What replaced it

A `dl` of four losses — tool name, what it does better, and no "closest
competitor" badge, no price column, no legend of coloured dots. The heading says
what the section is: "Four things other tools do better".

## The gaps are not lost

They are issues, not prose: #43 is the map, #44–#49 are the lanes (rule
enforcement, packages, language breadth, history, agent surface, guided tours).
Each lane carries the check that would prove it, which is what makes it a lane
rather than a wish.

## Failure modes

- Keeping "just one" price for credibility, and thereby owning the maintenance.
- Filing the lanes and letting them become the same argument in a different place.
